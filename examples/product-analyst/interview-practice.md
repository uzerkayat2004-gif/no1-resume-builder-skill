# Interview practice - Product Analyst

Use these prompts to practice truthful, conversational answers. The claim IDs are evidence anchors, not text to recite.

## Opening

1. Walk me through your background and why it is relevant to Product Analyst.
   - Build a 60-90 second answer from the summary and the two most relevant recent roles.

## Requirement questions

1. The role requires: SQL and product funnel analysis
   - Evidence status: supported
   - Evidence anchors: ev-tools, ev-funnel
2. The role requires: Experiment design and interpretation
   - Evidence status: supported
   - Evidence anchors: ev-experiment
3. The role requires: Marketplace pricing analytics
   - Evidence status: unsupported
   - Evidence anchors: none
   - Practice an honest gap response: acknowledge the gap, name adjacent evidence, and explain how you would close it.

## Resume deep dives

1. Walk me through this result: “Built a weekly activation funnel in SQL and Python that helped the product team identify two onboarding drop-off points.”
   - Evidence anchors: ev-funnel
   - Cover Situation, Task, Action, Result, and Reflection. Be ready to explain your exact contribution, trade-offs, and what you learned.
2. Walk me through this result: “Designed an experiment for a revised driver checklist; the confirmed release increased first-week completion from 62% to 71%.”
   - Evidence anchors: ev-experiment
   - Cover Situation, Task, Action, Result, and Reflection. Be ready to explain your exact contribution, trade-offs, and what you learned.
3. Walk me through this result: “Automated the monthly learner-retention report, reducing preparation time from two days to three hours.”
   - Evidence anchors: ev-automation
   - Cover Situation, Task, Action, Result, and Reflection. Be ready to explain your exact contribution, trade-offs, and what you learned.
4. Walk me through this result: “Partnered with product and curriculum teams to define adoption metrics for four new course features.”
   - Evidence anchors: ev-adoption
   - Cover Situation, Task, Action, Result, and Reflection. Be ready to explain your exact contribution, trade-offs, and what you learned.

## Reusable follow-ups

- What part did you personally own?
- What alternatives did you consider?
- How did you measure the result?
- What would you do differently?
- Which stakeholder disagreed, and how did you handle it?
- What evidence can you show or discuss without exposing confidential information?

## Practice rule

Do not memorize a fictional script. Practice the structure, facts, and evidence until you can explain them naturally.
