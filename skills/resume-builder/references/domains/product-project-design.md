# Product, project, program, and design

## Evidence to prioritize

- problem framing, discovery, prioritization, and decisions;
- roadmap, scope, dependencies, risks, budget, timeline, and quality;
- launches, adoption, accessibility, usability, service improvement, or stakeholder alignment;
- research, prototypes, portfolio artifacts, systems, and iteration;
- clear personal responsibility within cross-functional work.

## Questions

- Which customer, user, operational, or business problem mattered?
- What evidence changed the priority or design?
- Which decision did you own, recommend, or facilitate?
- What constraints, dependencies, risks, or disagreements did you handle?
- What happened after delivery, and how do you know?

## Useful sections

Summary; Skills/Methods; Experience; Selected Products, Programs, or Portfolio Projects; Education/Certifications.

Design resumes should link to a portfolio when relevant, but the resume must still explain the problem and contribution. Project/program resumes should clarify scope without turning into a task log.

## Cautions

- Do not attribute team outcomes entirely to one person.
- Do not report vanity metrics without relevance.
- Do not confuse participation with ownership.
- Do not use visual novelty that makes the application document hard to parse.
