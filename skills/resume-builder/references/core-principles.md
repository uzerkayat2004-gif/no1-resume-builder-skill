# Core principles

## Purpose

A resume is a selective evidence document for a particular opportunity. It is not a biography, a keyword container, a personal identity file, or proof that a candidate deserves an outcome.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use these source states:

- verified: transcribed faithfully from a supplied document, portfolio artifact, transcript, certificate, or another source the candidate has authorized;
- user-confirmed: stated and explicitly confirmed by the candidate;
- unverified: plausible or imported but not confirmed.

Verified means source-backed, not independently background-checked. Final resumes may use verified and user-confirmed claims, but ask the candidate to confirm material imported dates, numbers, scope, credentials, and ownership. Keep unverified claims in a question list or visibly marked draft.

When several sources conflict, do not silently choose one. Show the conflict and ask the candidate.

## Canonical record

Keep one career record with:

- facts and dates;
- evidence status and notes;
- a broad accomplishment bank;
- skills demonstrated by evidence;
- job-specific requirement mappings;
- variants that select and order the same facts differently.

Do not overwrite the master record with a tailored version.

## Good judgment

- Relevance beats completeness.
- Specificity beats grand language.
- Readability beats visual novelty for ordinary application documents.
- A meaningful non-numeric outcome beats an invented metric.
- Employer instructions beat generalized advice.
- Regional practices are context, not universal truths.
- A parser result is evidence about extraction, not a hiring prediction.

## Quality gate

Before finalizing:

1. Can the candidate defend every material statement in an interview?
2. Do dates, titles, scope, metrics, and education agree with the evidence?
3. Does the document show the most relevant evidence early?
4. Is each keyword used naturally and truthfully?
5. Are unsupported requirements visible rather than disguised?
6. Does text extraction preserve the content in logical order?
7. Can a human read it comfortably at normal zoom?
