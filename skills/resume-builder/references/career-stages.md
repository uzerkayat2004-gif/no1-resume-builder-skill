# Career-stage overlays

Apply one or more overlays after selecting the occupational domain.

## Student or first job

- Lead with education, licenses, projects, placements, internships, or relevant work according to the target.
- Convert coursework into evidence only when the candidate can describe what they built, analyzed, practiced, or learned.
- Include unrelated paid work when it demonstrates reliability, service, leadership, safety, or transferable responsibility.
- Never invent users, revenue, scale, or project adoption.

## Career changer

- Preserve the real chronology.
- Use a short summary to explain direction and transferable strengths.
- Emphasize adjacent tasks and evidence; label training and projects honestly.
- Do not rewrite an old title to look like the new profession.

## Returner or employment gap

- Do not hide dates through deceptive formatting.
- Include caregiving, volunteering, study, freelance work, or community leadership when the user wants and it is relevant.
- A gap explanation is optional on the resume; prepare a concise interview explanation.

## Freelancer, contractor, or portfolio worker

- Group work under a truthful self-employment or contract heading when it improves clarity.
- Name clients only when permitted.
- Show project scope, deliverables, repeat business, quality, and constraints.
- Distinguish completed, ongoing, and unpaid work.

## Executive or senior leader

- Lead with enterprise scope, strategic choices, operating model, talent leadership, governance, and durable outcomes.
- Distinguish personal decisions from organization-wide results.
- Use an executive summary and selected career evidence; do not turn the resume into a biography.
- Read references/domains/executive-leadership.md.

## Applicant with limited formal education

- Do not make education visually dominant unless required.
- Lead with licenses, trade qualifications, experience, reliability, safety, customer outcomes, or demonstrated skill.
- Never imply a degree or certification.

## Applicant with disabilities or protected circumstances

- Do not disclose health or disability information unless the user explicitly chooses to.
- Keep accommodation requests separate from the resume unless the application process requires otherwise.
