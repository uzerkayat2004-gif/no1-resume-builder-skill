# Writing and tailoring

## Requirement matrix

Convert the job posting into a compact matrix:

| Requirement | Priority | Evidence status | Claim IDs | Resume action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact requirement text | minimum/preferred/responsibility | supported/transferable/unsupported/unclear | IDs or none | emphasize, clarify, omit, or ask |

Keep unsupported minimum requirements visible in the working notes. Do not fabricate a match.

## Summary

Use a summary only when it adds a job-specific frame that the rest of the page cannot establish fast enough.

A useful summary normally contains:

- target professional identity;
- relevant experience scope;
- two or three differentiating capabilities;
- the domain or outcome the candidate is prepared to contribute to.

Avoid objectives about what the candidate hopes to receive, first-person filler, adjective strings, and unsupported seniority.

## Experience bullets

Start from evidence, not a formula:

1. What changed, shipped, improved, prevented, clarified, enabled, or decided?
2. What did the candidate personally do?
3. What method, skill, or judgment mattered?
4. What evidence shows the result or significance?

Possible evidence forms:

- quantitative outcome supplied by the candidate;
- scale, duration, frequency, budget, team, users, regions, or systems;
- quality or reliability change;
- time saved or risk reduced;
- adoption or stakeholder decision;
- launch, delivery, or process completion;
- complexity handled;
- customer, learner, patient, citizen, or operational outcome;
- published, approved, audited, or recognized work.

Do not turn every bullet into an identical Action–Metric sentence. Repetition sounds artificial and can erase important context.

## Page length

Do not enforce one page universally. Use one page when it carries the strongest relevant proof comfortably. Use two when removing the second page would delete material evidence or make the document unreadable. Require a clear context before exceeding two pages in a normal private-sector application.

Huntr's 2025 observational report analyzed 243,973 resumes and found two-page resumes common in its platform, with a small interview-rate advantage over one-page resumes and lower rates for three-plus pages. Its data is North-America- and tech-heavy, so treat the finding as directional.

## Skills

List only job-relevant capabilities the candidate can discuss or demonstrate. Prioritize hard and role-specific skills. Grouping is useful when it improves scanning, for example:

- Data: SQL, Python, experiment design
- Product: discovery, funnel analysis, roadmapping
- Platforms: Snowflake, Looker, Git

Avoid self-rated skill bars and unsupported proficiency labels.

## Common edits

- “Responsible for weekly reports” → identify what the reports enabled and what the candidate changed.
- “Worked with stakeholders” → identify which stakeholders, what decision, and the candidate's contribution.
- “Improved performance by 30%” → keep only if the baseline, metric, time period, and candidate contribution are confirmable.
- “Excellent communicator” → show a specific briefing, facilitation, alignment, or decision outcome.
