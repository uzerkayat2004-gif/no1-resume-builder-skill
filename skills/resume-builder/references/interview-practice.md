# Resume-grounded interview practice

## Purpose

Use the final resume and job requirement matrix to help the candidate retrieve real evidence under interview pressure. Do not write a fictional performance.

## Question set

Build a balanced set:

- opening: tell me about yourself; why this role/company;
- screening: availability, location, work authorization only when relevant and lawful;
- resume deep dives: each major recent claim;
- behavioral: collaboration, conflict, ambiguity, failure, feedback, ownership, prioritization;
- role-specific: methods, tools, decisions, trade-offs, and quality standards;
- gap questions: requirements that are transferable, unsupported, or unclear;
- candidate questions: role outcomes, manager expectations, team interfaces, hiring process.

## Evidence-linked answer outline

For each high-value question:

1. State the question.
2. Name the competency or requirement.
3. Cite the resume claim IDs.
4. Outline Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection when appropriate.
5. Mark facts that still need confirmation.
6. Add two likely follow-ups.

Keep the Action section specific to what the candidate did. Team outcomes are valid, but the candidate must distinguish team contribution from personal ownership.

## Honest gap answer

Use:

1. acknowledge the exact gap without apologizing excessively;
2. describe adjacent, verified experience;
3. explain the learning or execution approach;
4. give an example of learning a comparable skill when available;
5. ask a useful question about the employer's real need.

Never advise the candidate to imply experience they do not have.

## Practice method

- First pass: answer with notes and verify facts.
- Second pass: answer in 60–120 seconds without reading.
- Third pass: handle skeptical follow-ups.
- Fourth pass: shorten while preserving the evidence.
- Final pass: vary wording so it sounds conversational rather than memorized.

The OPM structured interview guide is useful because it emphasizes consistent questions and job-related competencies. Adapt that logic for practice; do not claim to know an employer's exact interview rubric.
